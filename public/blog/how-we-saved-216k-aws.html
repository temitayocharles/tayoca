How We Saved a Client $216K/yr on AWS

A FinOps case study: uncovering hidden cloud waste in a Series B fintech's AWS bill.

Request Your Free Audit →

The Challenge

A Series B fintech company with $40M ARR was growing rapidly, but their AWS spend was growing faster. They suspected inefficiencies but lacked the granularity to pinpoint where money was leaking. Their monthly AWS bill hovered around $70K, translating to ~$840K annually — a significant portion of their operating expenses. Engineering leadership needed clarity: were they overpaying, and if so, where?

Our Approach

We conducted our signature 30-minute FinOps audit. The client provided their detailed AWS CUR (Cost and Usage Report) CSV from the previous month. Using our proprietary analysis framework, we processed 1.2M line items across EC2, RDS, S3, Lambda, and data transfer services.

Our methodology focuses on eight high-impact areas: compute idle rightsizing, storage lifecycle, database optimization, reserved instance coverage, data transfer optimization, licensing efficiency, orphaned resources, and usage anomaly detection.

The Discovery

Within 48 hours, we delivered a prioritized 15-item remediation plan with projected ROI. The findings revealed systematic waste across multiple services:

  • Overprovisioned EC2 instances: 42% of running instances were utilization <20% CPU. Right-sizing alone saved $78K/year.
  • Unused reserved instances: $34K/year in RI fees for instances that had been terminated months prior but not removed from the RI portfolio.
  • S3 storage class inefficiencies: 18TB of infrequently accessed data stored in Standard tier instead of IA or Glacier, costing $22K/year extra.
  • Data transfer charges: Cross-AZ traffic within VPCs costing $19K/year due to poor subnet architecture.
  • Lambda timeout configurations: 27 functions with 30-second timeouts averaging 1.2s execution — unnecessary concurrency limits costing $14K/year.
  • Orphaned snapshots and AMIs: 1.4TB of outdated ESB snapshots retained beyond compliance needs, incurring $12K/year in storage fees.
  • RDS storage over-allocation: Auto-scaling thresholds set too high, leading to $9K/year in provisioned IOPS that were never utilized.

The total identified annual savings: $216K — exactly 25.7% of their current AWS spend.

The Implementation

We didn't just hand over a report and leave. Our team worked alongside the client's platform engineering squad to implement the top 10 items over four weeks:

  1. Automated EC2 rightsizing using AWS Compute Optimizer + Lambda remediation.
  2. RI portfolio cleanup and automated expiry alerts via Config rules.
  3. S3 lifecycle policies transitioning data to IA after 30 days, Glacier after 365 days.
  4. VPC traffic flow analysis and subnet consolidation to eliminate cross-AZ fees.
  5. Lambda right-sizing based on actual invocation duration percentiles.
  6. Snapshot cleanup lifecycle with retention policies enforced via AWS Backup.
  7. RDS storage auto-scaling threshold adjustments based on actual utilization metrics.
  8. Custom CloudWatch dashboard for ongoing waste detection.
  9. Monthly FinOps review cadence established with engineering and finance stakeholders.

By week six, verified savings began appearing in the AWS Cost Explorer. After 90 days, the client confirmed a sustained run-rate reduction of $216K/year.

The Impact

Beyond the direct cost savings, the engagement delivered secondary benefits:

  • Engineering velocity: Eliminated low-priority infrastructure tickets, freeing ~10 hours/week for feature work.
  • Improved observability: Tagging compliance increased from 45% to 92%, enabling better cost allocation.
  • Culture shift: Weekly FinOps reviews created shared ownership of cloud economics between engineering and finance.
