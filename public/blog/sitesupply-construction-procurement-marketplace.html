Construction procurement should not run on quote chasing.

Tayoca is working with a B2B SaaS client to bring SiteSupply to market, a procurement marketplace built around requirements, supplier matching, RFQs, comparable quotes and documented award decisions.

The procurement problem is not a lack of phone numbers

Construction buyers already know how to call suppliers. The costly part is everything around that call: finding the right supplier for the exact requirement, repeating the same request, comparing quote formats that do not line up, checking delivery terms, tracking who responded, and preserving the final decision for the project record.

A supplier directory does not solve that operating problem. SiteSupply is built around the procurement transaction itself.

One request, matched suppliers, comparable quotes

The SiteSupply workflow is designed to move a buyer through a complete procurement cycle:

  1. Create a project with location, dates and requirements.
  2. Add category-specific material, equipment or site-service line items.
  3. Match suppliers by category, radius and availability.
  4. Send an RFQ to relevant suppliers.
  5. Receive structured quotes with comparable commercial fields.
  6. Compare delivered cost, timing, distance, rating and other visible factors.
  7. Select a supplier and keep the award decision attached to the procurement record.

For suppliers, the opportunity is qualified demand

Suppliers can define what they sell, where they serve, their service areas and relevant commercial information. The goal is not to spray every supplier with every request. The platform is designed to route RFQs toward suppliers whose category and coverage match the buyer's need.

SiteSupply currently supports procurement categories that include aggregates, equipment rental, site services, freight and hauling, landscaping and grounds, irrigation and drainage, erosion control, fuel and lubricants, fencing, dumpsters, and diesel and fuel tanks.

Why Tayoca is talking about SiteSupply before a formal launch

A B2B marketplace does not become useful because a ribbon is cut. It becomes useful when the right buyers and suppliers are already aware, already evaluating the workflow, and already building activity before the formal launch moment.

That is why visibility starts now. SiteSupply gives Tayoca a concrete example of the kind of operating software we want to help bring into the market: software that turns fragmented work into a measurable, governable business process.

Three groups should act now

  • Construction suppliers: join early, complete verification and compete for relevant RFQs as demand enters the marketplace.
  • Buyers and project teams: use SiteSupply to search categories, post requests and compare structured supplier responses.
  • Organizations building serious SaaS or operational platforms: talk to Tayoca about the architecture, automation, reliability, analytics and operating controls required to take a product from idea to production.
